## AENLSB0A9C01001 - Anti-Corrosion Treatment
Path: ./source-mirror/SWIFT_A2L310_A2L412_A2L414/xml/AENLSB0A/AENLSB0A9C01001.xml
Tree scopes: bookmarks: 9 - Body, Cab and Accessories > 9L - Paint / Coatings > General Description
Outgoing references: none

Content:
Anti-Corrosion Treatment
Failure to follow standard shop practices when performing the operations described below, particularly wearing eye protector, may result in a personal injury.
Follow standard shop practices when performing anti-corrosion treatment.
Steel sheets used for panels of this vehicle are given the following rustproof treatments.
Electro-galvanization on both surfaces for all steel sheets
Cathodic electro-deposited primer coating for all steel sheets
Rustproof wax coating on inside surfaces of door and side sill panels that are exposed to staying moisture
Vinyl coating on floor panel underside and wheel house inside surfaces
Sealer application to door panel hems, panel-to-panel joints in engine compartment and other similar panel portions
The following describes the points that should be respected during the painting and rustproof coating involved in vehicle body repair operations.
Follow manufacturer’s instructions when using any types of paint and sealing material.
When replacing panels, repairing damage to them, or doing any other operations that disturb rustproof treatment of panels, it is essential to recoat affected surfaces with rustproof primers after thoroughly cleaning the areas.
Sealer application and rustproof waxing should be performed only after application of rustproof primer.
Apply sealer to the specified areas such as hems, overlap joints and seams during panel replacement and repair to prevent entry of dust and provide anti-corrosion barriers. Select a quality sealer that is adequate in flexibility and paint adhesion characteristic for application to these areas.
To fill open joints, use a caulking material.
When painting repaired areas, use techniques generally accepted for metal surface preparation, undercoating (vinyl coating) and color painting.
For places where ordinary undercoating materials are difficult to use, such as door and side sill insides, use rustproof wax, a penetrative compound.
When applying sealer during undercoating operation, avoid applying sealer to engine related components, strut/shock absorber mountings and rotating components. Also avoid blocking body drain holes with coating or sealing materials.
Application of anti-corrosion and sealing materials should be performed in the following procedure:
Clean and prepare metal surface.
Apply primer.
Apply sealer (for any originally sealed areas).
Apply color paint to necessary areas such as hem flanges, exposed joints and underbody components.
Apply rustproof wax (penetrative compound).
Apply undercoating (vinyl coating).
Plastic parts painting
On plastic parts, those made of hard Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS) can be coated with general acrylic lacquers in the following procedure without need for using primer.
Wash part to be painted in cleaning solvent.
Apply acrylic lacquer to part.
Dry part for the time specified by lacquer manufacturer. Proper drying temperature is between 60 and 70°C (140 and 158°F).
Reference
Parts made of Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS) can be distinguished from those made of other plastic materials, such as polypropylene and vinyl, using the following method.
Cut small piece of material from invisible area of plastic part to be tested using a sharp blade.
Hold the cut piece in pincers and put it over a flame.
If the piece burns generating very black smoke while leaving residue suspending in air, the plastic part is made of Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS). (If it is made of polypropylene, the smoke generated during burning is not distinctively visible.)

## AENLSB0AA111001 - Cruise Control System Construction
Path: ./source-mirror/SWIFT_A2L310_A2L412_A2L414/xml/AENLSB0A/AENLSB0AA111001.xml
Tree scopes: bookmarks: 10 - Control Systems > 10A - Cruise Control System > General Description > Cruise Control
Outgoing references: none

Content:
Cruise Control System Construction
Cruise control system is a device which maintains a preset vehicle speed while driving at a high speed, e.g. on a highway. It allows the driver to drive at a constant speed of 40 km/h (25 mile/h) or higher without depressing the accelerator pedal. The system also has functions to change vehicle speed without operating the accelerator pedal (SET/– and RES/+ switches), cancel cruise control (CANCEL switch) and automatically resume driving at the speed in memory when cruise control is reactivated (RES/+ switch).
The system mainly consists of electric throttle body assembly, ECM, cruise control switches (CRUISE (main) switch, SET/– switch, RES/+ switch and CANCEL switch), etc.

## AENLSB0AA111002 - Description of Cruise Control System Components and Functions
Path: ./source-mirror/SWIFT_A2L310_A2L412_A2L414/xml/AENLSB0A/AENLSB0AA111002.xml
Tree scopes: bookmarks: 10 - Control Systems > 10A - Cruise Control System > General Description > Cruise Control
Outgoing references: none

Content:
Description of Cruise Control System Components and Functions
Component
Function
ECM and electric throttle body
ECM performs centralized control over all functions including setting a constant speed, resuming it, setting coast, and canceling cruise control minimum limit speed.
ECM controls electric throttle valve opening to keep actual vehicle speed at set (target) speed.
CRUISE (main) switch
This switch has a momentary contact type button to turn cruise control function ON and OFF.
SET/– switch
When this switch is pressed (ON) and then released (OFF) while vehicle is running at a speed of 40 km/h (25 mile/h) or higher, vehicle speed at that OFF moment is stored in memory and it is maintained (constant cruising).
When this switch is continuously pressed (ON) during constant cruising, vehicle speed will continually decrease as long as switch is ON. When it is released (OFF), vehicle speed at that moment is stored in memory and vehicle starts constant cruising.
Pressing this switch (ON) during constant cruising decreases vehicle speed according to number of times the switch is pressed.
RES/+ switch
When this switch is pressed (ON) during constant cruising, vehicle speed will continually increase as long as switch is ON. When it is released (OFF), vehicle speed at that moment is stored in memory and vehicle starts constant cruising. If vehicle speed is higher than 40 km/h (25 mile/h) after cruise control is canceled, pressing this switch ON momentarily will resume vehicle running at the speed.
Pressing this switch (ON) during constant cruising increases vehicle speed according to number of times the switch is pressed.
CANCEL switch
When this switch is pressed (ON), cruise control (throttle valve control) is canceled.
Wheel speed sensor (vehicle speed signal)
ECM receives speed sensor signal from ESP ® control module through CAN communication and calculates vehicle speed using that signal.
Brake light switch
Brake light switch has 2 contact points. One contact point closes when brake pedal is depressed to light brake light and provide a voltage signal to the ECM.
The other contact point (brake pedal position switch) opens when brake pedal is depressed to shut off power to cruise control of ECM, thereby canceling cruise control (throttle valve control).
This switch is installed to cancel cruise control.
Clutch pedal position switch (M/T model)
When clutch pedal is depressed, clutch pedal position switch closes and provides a ground signal to ECM.
ECM cancels cruise control (throttle valve control) when this signal is inputted.
TCM (A/T model)
TCM transmits transmission shift position signal to ECM through CAN communication.
“CRUISE” indicator
In the state with ignition “ON” and cruise control system in OFF, pressing CRUISE (main) switch once and releasing it will activate the cruise control system and ECM will cause indicator to light up.
“SET” indicator
Lights up when cruise control (throttle valve control) is functioning.

Content:
Description of Cruise Control System Cancel Conditions
Constant cruising is canceled under one of the following conditions.
CRUISE (main) switch is turned OFF.
*Vehicle speed becomes lower than minimum operating speed (36 km/h (22 mile/h)).
*Actual vehicle speed is continuously lower than set vehicle speed by a specified amount.
*Brake pedal is depressed, that is; brake light switch is turned ON.
*Clutch pedal is depressed, that is; clutch pedal position switch is turned ON. (M/T model)
*Gear shift lever is in 1st or 2nd position. (M/T model)
*Select lever is shifted to other than “D” and “M” ranges. (A/T model)
*Select lever is in “M” range and gear position is in 1st or 2nd position. (A/T model)
*Gear position is changed. (M/T model)
*CANCEL switch is turned ON.
*ESP ® is operating.
Acceleration or deceleration is out of the specified value.
When constant cruising is canceled due to a condition with * (asterisk), vehicle speed before cancellation can be resumed by operating RES/+ switch, provided that vehicle speed is higher than 40 km/h (25 mile/h).
