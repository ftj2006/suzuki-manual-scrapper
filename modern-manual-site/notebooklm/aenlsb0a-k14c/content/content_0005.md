Gasoline Particulate Filter (GPF) Description
Gasoline particulate filter (GPF) is a device to constitute the exhaust system.
GPF is canned together with catalytic converter and located under the catalytic converter.
GPF collects particulate matter (PM) from emission and reduces harmful substance in the emission.
A pressure sensor is configured to monitor pressure difference between the upper and lower reaches of GPF for clogging. Further, ECM estimates collected soot volume in GPF monitoring the engine condition.
Under a normal driving condition, the soot is burned off naturally if GPF is in higher temperature than the specification and oxygen is supplied during fuel cut operation etc.
If ECM estimates clogging of GPF exceeding the specified limit, ignition timing is retarded to raise exhaust temperature and accelerates forced burning off of the soot.
And, if the clogging is not eliminated even after the forced burning off and estimated PM accumulation calculated by ECM is higher than the specification, ECM turns on GPF warning light in the combination meter and indicates “DRIVE TO REGENERATE GPF” on information display to warn the driver of clogged GPF and urge high speed driving to raise exhaust temperature.
Further, if the clogging is not eliminated yet and pressure difference of exhaust between upper and lower of GPF, or estimated soot volume in GPF calculated by ECM is higher than the specification, ECM turns on GPF warning light and indicates “WARNING GPF SYSTEM” on information display to warn the driver of clogged GPF and urge to enter the vehicle for servicing.

Gasoline Particulate Filter (GPF) Regeneration Description
General Description
Soot in the GPF is burned off naturally when the GPF temperature is higher than specified temperature and fuel cut is performed at a speed higher than the specification (on condition that emission contains oxygen).
Forced Regeneration
When ECM judges that the accumulation of the soot has exceeded the specification, ignition timing is retarded to raise GPF temperature so that the GPF is regenerated by burning off the accumulated soot.
Depending on the state of the soot elimination, further rise of exhaust temperature is required. When the temperature rise is necessary, ECM turns on GPF warning light in the combination meter, indicates “DRIVE TO REGENERATE GPF” on information display and requests the driver to fulfill the following driving.
Vehicle speed: 50 km/h (31 mile/h) or higher
Engine speed: 2,000 rpm or more
After-Sales Regeneration
Regeneration of GPF by burning off the accumulated soot using SUZUKI scan tool is called “After-sales Regeneration”.
ECM informs necessity of “After-sales Regeneration” by turning on GPF warning light, indicating “DRIVE TO REGENERATE GPF” on information display. At the same time, ECM detects DTC P2463 or P24A4.
While performing “After-sales Regeneration”, GPF temperature rises by raising engine speed and retarding ignition timing. Further, lean A/F ratio is forcedly generated for burning off the accumulated soot.
In either case, “After-sales Regeneration” is necessary when ECM is replaced.

EGR (Exhaust Gas Recirculation) System Description
In this system, purified exhaust gas (1) passed through GPF flows into EGR cooler for cooling and recirculated into combustion chamber (2) through the intake manifold (3). This cooled exhaust gas in the combustion chamber improves the engine performance reducing abnormal combustion (knocking), pumping loss and fuel consumption.
4.
Fresh air
Operation Description
The system mainly consists of EGR pipes, EGR cooler (2) and EGR valve (1).
EGR valve is controlled by ECM corresponding to signals from ECT sensor, IAT sensor, barometric pressure sensor, etc., and the driving conditions.
EGR valve is composed of a motor (3), valve (4), spring (5), bushing (6), etc.
Purified exhaust gas downstream of GPF flows into EGR cooler and, the cooled exhaust gas recirculates in the combustion chamber. Through this recirculation, engine performance is substantially improved reducing piston knocking, pumping loss and fuel consumption, not to mention reduction of NOx.
7.
Inner fin
8.
Exhaust gas
9.
Engine coolant
EGR flow rate is electrically regulated by ECM.
When ECM stops the power supply to EGR valve, the internal valve remains in the position and gets free.
Under any one of the following conditions, ECM closes EGR valve.
When engine coolant temperature is lower or higher than specified value.
When intake air temperature is lower or higher than specified value.
When engine is running under supercharged condition.
When vehicle is stopped (at idling).

Content:
Engine Electrical Devices Description
Various sensors for the engine control in this model are outlined as follows.
MAF Sensor with IAT Sensor-1
A thermistor in IAT sensor No.1 (1) incorporated in MAF sensor mounted on air cleaner outlet changes resistance corresponding to intake air temperature and the resistance converted to signal is outputted to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates intake air temperature.
MAF sensor is consists of a heater (2) on a hot film and 2 temperature sensors (3) arranged in upstream side and downstream side of the intake air [a].
When air flows across the hot film, the upstream side sensor cools differently from the downstream side. The temperature difference between the 2 proportionally increases with increase of the mass airflow and the value is converted to digital signal and outputted to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates mass airflow.
[b]:
A part of intake air
Boost Pressure Sensor with IAT Sensor-2
A thermistor in IAT sensor-2 incorporated in boost pressure sensor mounted on intercooler outlet pipe downstream of the intercooler generates voltage signal corresponding to intake air temperature and sends it to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates supercharged intake air temperature.
A membrane diaphragm sensor incorporated in boost pressure sensor generates voltage signal corresponding to intake air pressure and sends it to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates supercharged intake air pressure.
MAP Sensor
The sensor is mounted on intake manifold.
A pressure responsive silicon membrane incorporated in diaphragm generates voltage signal corresponding to intake pressure (absolute pressure) and the signal is inputted to ECM. Based on the signal, ECM calculates manifold absolute pressure.
TP Sensor (Main / Sub)
The sensor is mounted on electric throttle body.
A noncontact type sensor with Hall IC generates dual voltage signals in response to throttle valve opening and these signals, main and sub, are inputted to ECM.
Based on the signals, ECM calculates opening degree of the throttle valve.
HO2S-1
HO2S-1 is mounted on the upper part of the three-way catalytic converter.
The sensor is composed of a platinum coated zirconia element which generates electromotive force sensing difference of oxygen concentration. Voltage signal corresponding to variation of oxygen concentration in exhaust gas before flowing into catalyst is inputted to ECM.
Judging from signals of HO2S-1 and HO2S-2, ECM controls A/F (air fuel ratio) to maintain purification rate of exhaust gas in high level for stabile emissions. A heater is incorporated in oxygen sensor for quick startup and maintenance of activation.
HO2S-2
HO2S-2 is mounted on the lower part of the three-way catalytic converter.
The sensor is composed of a platinum coated zirconia element which generates electromotive force sensing difference of oxygen concentration. Voltage signal corresponding to variation of oxygen concentration in exhaust gas after flowing out of catalyst is inputted to ECM.
Judging from signals of HO2S-1 and HO2S-2, ECM controls A/F (air fuel ratio) to maintain purification rate of exhaust gas in high level for stabile emissions. A heater is incorporated in oxygen sensor for quick startup and maintenance of activation.
APP Sensor (Main / Sub)
The sensor is mounted on accelerator pedal.
A noncontact type sensor applying electromagnetic induction detects depressing degree of accelerator pedal.
Dual voltage signals, main and sub, are inputted to ECM. Based on the signals, ECM controls throttle valve opening activating throttle motor.
Oil Temperature Sensor
A thermistor incorporated in the sensor changes electrical resistance corresponding to engine oil temperature and the resistance converted to voltage is inputted to ECM. Based on the voltage signal, ECM calculates oil temperature.
ECT Sensor
The sensor is mounted on thermostat case.
A thermistor incorporated in the sensor changes electrical resistance corresponding to temperature and the resistance converted to voltage is inputted to ECM. Based on the voltage signal, ECM calculates coolant temperature.
CMP Sensor
The sensor is mounted on the thermostat case.
Magnetic variation induced by a signal rotor press-fitted on camshaft end is converted to voltage signal in the sensor and the signal is inputted to ECM.
Based on CMP signal and CKP signal, ECM reads each piston position in cylinder and controls fuel injection and ignition timing. Further, detecting open/close timing of intake valves, ECM controls VVT operation.
CKP Sensor
The sensor is mounted on cylinder block near the crank pulley at intake manifold side.
Magnetic variation induced by a sensor plate installed on crankshaft is converted to voltage signal in the sensor and the signal is inputted to ECM. Using the signal, ECM calculates engine speed.
Based on CMP signal and CKP signal, ECM reads each piston position in cylinder and controls fuel injection and ignition timing.
Knock Sensor
The sensor is mounted on the upper part of the cylinder block at intake side.
A piezoelectric ceramic element incorporated in the sensor generates electromotive force in proportion to degree of shock and the generated voltage signal is inputted to ECM. Using the signal, ECM detects piston knocking.

## AENLSB0A1321002 - Engine Electrical Devices Description
Path: ./source-mirror/SWIFT_A2L310_A2L412_A2L414/xml/AENLSB0A/AENLSB0A1321002.xml

Content:
Engine Electrical Devices Description
Various sensors for the engine control in this model are outlined as follows.
MAF Sensor with IAT Sensor-1
A thermistor in IAT sensor No.1 (1) incorporated in MAF sensor mounted on air cleaner outlet changes resistance corresponding to intake air temperature and the resistance converted to signal is outputted to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates intake air temperature.
MAF sensor is consists of a heater (2) on a hot film and 2 temperature sensors (3) arranged in upstream side and downstream side of the intake air [a].
When air flows across the hot film, the upstream side sensor cools differently from the downstream side. The temperature difference between the 2 proportionally increases with increase of the mass airflow and the value is converted to digital signal and outputted to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates mass airflow.
[b]:
A part of intake air
Boost Pressure Sensor with IAT Sensor-2
A thermistor in IAT sensor-2 incorporated in boost pressure sensor mounted on intercooler outlet pipe downstream of the intercooler generates voltage signal corresponding to intake air temperature and sends it to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates supercharged intake air temperature.
A membrane diaphragm sensor incorporated in boost pressure sensor generates voltage signal corresponding to intake air pressure and sends it to ECM. Receiving the signal, ECM calculates supercharged intake air pressure.
MAP Sensor
The sensor is mounted on intake manifold.
A pressure responsive silicon membrane incorporated in diaphragm generates voltage signal corresponding to intake pressure (absolute pressure) and the signal is inputted to ECM. Based on the signal, ECM calculates manifold absolute pressure.
TP Sensor (Main / Sub)
The sensor is mounted on electric throttle body.
A noncontact type sensor with Hall IC generates dual voltage signals in response to throttle valve opening and these signals, main and sub, are inputted to ECM.
Based on the signals, ECM calculates opening degree of the throttle valve.
A/F Sensor
The sensor is mounted on the upper part of the three-way catalytic converter with GPF and contains a sensor heater and sensor element.
The sensor element is of a laminated structure which consists of a zirconia element, detection chamber and oxygen reference chamber. ECM detects the oxygen concentration (A/F ratio) of the exhaust emission from “LEAN” to “RICH” in proportion using the zirconia characteristic.
The sensor heater maintains the sensor element temperature at approx. 780 °C (1,436 °F) constantly by the duty control signal to activate the sensor element.
HO2S
HO2S is mounted on the lower part of the three-way catalytic converter with GPF.
The sensor is composed of a platinum coated zirconia element which generates electromotive force sensing difference of oxygen concentration. Voltage signal corresponding to variation of oxygen concentration in exhaust gas after flowing out of catalyst is inputted to ECM.
Judging from signals of A/F sensor and HO2S, ECM controls A/F (air fuel ratio) to maintain purification rate of exhaust gas in high level for stabile emissions. A heater is incorporated in oxygen sensor for quick startup and maintenance of activation.
APP Sensor (Main / Sub)
The sensor is mounted on accelerator pedal.
A noncontact type sensor applying electromagnetic induction detects depressing degree of accelerator pedal.
Dual voltage signals, main and sub, are inputted to ECM. Based on the signals, ECM controls throttle valve opening activating throttle motor.
Oil Pressure and Temperature Sensor
A thermistor (temperature sensor) and a piezo resistive pressure sensor are integrated in the oil pressure and temperature sensor. Corresponding to engine oil pressure and temperature, each sensor changes electrical resistance and the resistance is outputted as electric value. And, the electric value is converted to pulse signal and inputted to ECM. Based on the signal, ECM calculates oil pressure and temperature.
ECT Sensor
The sensor is mounted on thermostat case.
A thermistor incorporated in the sensor changes electrical resistance corresponding to temperature and the resistance converted to voltage is inputted to ECM. Based on the voltage signal, ECM calculates coolant temperature.
CMP Sensor
Intake side sensor is mounted on the thermostat case.
Exhaust side sensor is mounted on the end of cylinder head.
Magnetic variation induced by a signal rotor press-fitted on camshaft end is converted to voltage signal in the sensor and the signal is inputted to ECM.
Based on CMP signal and CKP signal, ECM reads each piston position in cylinder and controls fuel injection and ignition timing. Further, detecting open/close timing of intake valves, ECM controls VVT operation.
CKP Sensor
The sensor is mounted on cylinder block near the crank pulley at intake manifold side.
Magnetic variation induced by a sensor plate installed on crankshaft is converted to voltage signal in the sensor and the signal is inputted to ECM. Using the signal, ECM calculates engine speed.
Based on CMP signal and CKP signal, ECM reads each piston position in cylinder and controls fuel injection and ignition timing.
Knock Sensor
The sensor is mounted on the upper part of the cylinder block at intake side.
A piezoelectric ceramic element incorporated in the sensor generates electromotive force in proportion to degree of shock and the generated voltage signal is inputted to ECM. Using the signal, ECM detects piston knocking.
